A teenage girl (early-to-mid teens) was admitted with a 4-day history of sore throat and
fever, and a 1-day history of yellowing of her skin and eyes. Two months earlier she had
been diagnosed with a hyperthyroid disorder based on elevated thyroid hormone levels, a
suppressed thyroid-stimulating hormone level, and a significantly elevated thyroid
receptor antibody titer; she had since been taking daily methimazole for over 50 days
without recent follow-up blood testing. On admission she was febrile (39.3°C), tachycardic
(133 bpm), tachypneic, with borderline low blood pressure and a low body mass index. She
appeared restless and diaphoretic, with mild eye bulging and an enlarged thyroid gland.
Examination also showed jaundice of the skin and sclera, pharyngeal erythema, and palpable
enlarged cervical lymph nodes. Blood tests showed a mildly elevated white cell count with
an increased lymphocyte percentage and atypical lymphocytes on peripheral smear and bone
marrow examination; a pharyngeal swab and serology indicated a recent Epstein-Barr virus
infection. Liver enzymes, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin (predominantly direct) were
markedly elevated, with elevated total bile acids. Coagulation studies showed mildly
prolonged APTT and elevated D-dimer and fibrinogen degradation products, with normal
PT/INR. Abdominal ultrasound showed an enlarged liver and spleen. Tests for hepatotropic
viruses and autoimmune liver disease were negative. Repeat thyroid testing showed markedly
suppressed TSH with very high free T3 and T4 and a markedly elevated thyroid receptor
antibody level; thyroid scan showed an enlarged, hypervascular gland with increased tracer
uptake. Inflammatory markers including interleukin-2 receptor, interleukin-8,
interleukin-10, and TNF-alpha were elevated.
